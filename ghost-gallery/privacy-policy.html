GHOST GALLERY

ON-DEVICE. ALWAYS.
EFFECTIVE AS OF AUGUST 17, 2026

Dronova Tech ("we," "our," or "us") built Ghost Gallery to work entirely on your device. This policy explains, plainly, what the app touches — and just as importantly, what it never does.

1. Information We Access

a. Screenshots on Your Device

Ghost Gallery reads your device's Screenshots folder through Android's MediaStore to build a local index. Depending on your Android version, this uses the READ_MEDIA_IMAGES or READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE permission; on Android 14 and later, you can instead grant access to just a selected subset of photos.

b. On-Device Analysis

Each screenshot is analyzed on your device using on-device machine learning (text recognition and image labeling) to detect duplicates, expired verification codes, chat screenshots, and searchable content for the Museum. None of this analysis is ever sent anywhere — it runs locally, using models bundled with the app.

c. Sensitive Content

If analysis detects something that looks like a one-time passcode or card number, it is masked before being stored, and is kept only long enough to power the "delete expired codes" suggestion.

2. What We Never Do

Ghost Gallery has no INTERNET permission in its Android manifest. This isn't just a policy promise — the operating system itself blocks the app from making any network connection at all. There is no cloud storage, no account, no analytics SDK, and no advertising SDK, even by accident.

  • No AdMob or any other advertising SDK.
  • No Firebase Analytics, Crashlytics, or other tracking/measurement service.
  • No account, login, or personal identifiers of any kind.

3. Where Your Data Lives

Everything Ghost Gallery generates — the screenshot index, Museum entries, and deletion history — stays in a local app database on your device. Android's automatic cloud backup is explicitly disabled for this data, specifically because the index contains text read out of your screenshots. Uninstalling the app removes this data completely, because it was never stored anywhere else.

4. Notifications

Ghost Gallery can optionally remind you when it's a good time to review clutter. This uses Android's notification permission, requested only when you enable the reminder — never up front — and nothing about your notification preferences leaves your device.

5. Sharing a Screenshot

If you choose to share a screenshot from Ghost Gallery to another app, Ghost Gallery strips the image's EXIF metadata (such as embedded location data) before handing it over, and grants that one destination app a temporary, revocable link to that single file. Ghost Gallery does not upload the file itself — what happens after you share is governed by the receiving app's own privacy policy.

6. Deleting Screenshots

Screenshots you remove are held in an in-app bin first. Nothing is permanently deleted until you confirm Android's own system deletion prompt. On Android 11 and later, deleted items also pass through the system-level Trash, giving you a further 30-day window to recover them before they're gone for good. This is standard Android behavior, not something Ghost Gallery controls beyond making the request.

7. Children's Privacy

Ghost Gallery is a general-purpose utility and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age — the app has no way to collect personal information in the first place, since nothing it processes ever leaves your device.

8. Your Choices

You can revoke any permission Ghost Gallery uses at any time in Android's system settings. Uninstalling the app deletes all data associated with it immediately, since none of it exists anywhere else.

9. Changes to This Policy

If Ghost Gallery's data practices ever change, we will update this page and revise the effective date above.
